Product Vision and Strategic Alignment :: Strategic alignment connects the product vision to company goals so teams share priorities. Without alignment, two teams can build excellent features that pull the company in opposite directions. Review the link between vision and goals every quarter.
Strategic Product Planning :: Planning sets bets and sequencing across quarters and years, balancing growth, quality and team capacity. A plan should name the outcome each bet should move and what evidence would make you stop it.
Product Positioning :: Positioning defines for whom the product is, which category it belongs to, and why it is better than the alternatives. A clear positioning statement lets sales, marketing and product describe the same thing.
Product Differentiation :: Differentiation is a valued advantage competitors cannot easily copy, such as proprietary data, deep workflow integration or a trusted brand. A feature anyone can copy is parity, not differentiation.
Product Strategy Frameworks :: Frameworks such as Playing to Win ask where to play and how to win. Good Strategy separates diagnosis, guiding policy and coherent actions. Wardley maps show how components evolve over time. Use one framework consistently rather than many at once.
Product Roadmap Development :: A roadmap communicates intent over time. Show problems and outcomes rather than dated promises for features. Mark what is committed, planned and exploratory, so stakeholders understand the uncertainty.
Outcome-Based Roadmapping :: An outcome roadmap groups work under the customer or business outcome it should change, such as reducing onboarding time by 30%. Teams then compete on ideas for that outcome rather than delivering a fixed list of features.
OKRs and KPIs :: OKRs set ambitious objectives with measurable key results for a period. KPIs track ongoing health, such as uptime or monthly active users. Use OKRs for change you are pursuing and KPIs for the health you must maintain.
North Star Metric :: The north star is the single measure that best captures the value customers receive, such as weekly songs played per listener. It leads revenue and guides the team. Support it with input metrics the team can directly influence.
Product Portfolio Management :: Portfolio management allocates investment across products or bets by stage, risk and expected return. A healthy portfolio mixes proven products that fund growth with a few experiments that could become the next major line.
Feature Prioritisation :: Prioritisation orders work by value, effort, risk and strategic fit using explicit criteria. Write the criteria down so the ranking can be challenged on the inputs rather than on personal opinion.
RICE Framework :: RICE scores an initiative as (Reach x Impact x Confidence) divided by Effort. Example: reaching 5,000 users with impact 2, confidence 80% and effort 2 person-months gives (5,000 x 2 x 0.8) / 2 = 4,000. Use the score to compare items, not to decide alone.
MoSCoW Framework :: MoSCoW sorts requirements into Must, Should, Could and Won't-have for this release. It fixes scope early. Teams often label too many items as Must, so ask what fails if the item is removed.
Kano Model :: The Kano model separates basic expectations, performance features that scale satisfaction, and delighters that create surprise. Basic expectations must work or users leave. Delighters should be added after the basics are reliable.
ICE Scoring :: ICE scores Impact, Confidence and Ease from 1 to 10 and combines them. It is quick for early ideas. Its weakness is subjectivity, so calibrate scores by comparing two or three items the whole team already understands.
Impact vs Effort Matrix :: A two-by-two grid places items as quick wins (high impact, low effort), big bets, fill-ins and time sinks. It makes trade-offs visible in a single meeting. Plot items as a group discussion, not as one person's view.
Opportunity Scoring :: Opportunity scoring rates how important each customer need is and how satisfied customers are with current solutions. Needs that are important but poorly served point to opportunities. It works best with a survey of many customers.
Stakeholder Prioritisation :: Stakeholders bring competing requests from sales, support and executives. Agree the criteria first, then score requests together. Show how each request ranks, so people see the trade-off rather than a decision made in private.
Strategic Trade-Offs :: Strategy requires saying no. Trade-offs include speed against quality, breadth against depth and building now against later. Record the reasoning and the conditions under which the decision would change.
Product Investment Decisions :: Investment decisions weigh expected value, strategic fit, evidence and option value, which is the value of keeping choices open. Stage investment so a bet earns more funding only after it hits defined evidence milestones.
