Product Leadership Fundamentals :: Product leaders set direction, build clarity and accountability, and grow others while keeping the customer outcome in view. Leadership is measured by what the team can do without the leader.
Stakeholder Mapping :: List stakeholders by influence and interest, then decide how to engage each. An executive with high influence and low interest needs short, decision-focused updates.
Stakeholder Communication :: Tailored, regular updates state decisions, risks and requests clearly. Stakeholders trust a PM who reports bad news early.
Influence Without Authority :: Gain support through evidence, relationships, shared goals and understanding of others' incentives. Most PM work happens without direct authority over the people involved.
Cross-Functional Leadership :: Align product, engineering, design, data, sales and operations on one outcome. Shared goals and a common view of the data reduce conflict.
Engineering Collaboration :: Involve engineers early, respect constraints, and agree trade-offs on scope and quality together. An engineer's estimate of risk is valuable input, not an obstacle.
Design Collaboration :: Co-own the problem with designers, share research and critique work against goals rather than taste. Good critique asks what the user is trying to do.
Data Science Collaboration :: Frame the problem together, agree success metrics and evaluation, and share an understanding of model limits. Data scientists know what the data can and cannot support.
Executive Communication :: Lead with the conclusion, give the key numbers and state the decision needed. Keep it short, and prepare for the question you least want to be asked.
Product Presentations :: Structure the talk around the audience's question: context, insight, recommendation and next steps. Show the evidence behind each claim.
Negotiation :: Reach agreement by understanding interests, creating options and trading what each side values less for what it values more. Negotiate scope and date together, not separately.
Conflict Resolution :: Surface the real disagreement, use shared facts and refocus on the goal. Most product conflicts are about different assumptions about the user.
Decision-Making :: A good decision has a clear owner, defined criteria, enough evidence for the risk involved and a written record. Decide at the lowest level that has the information needed.
Product Reviews :: Scheduled reviews of progress, metrics and risks with leadership. Bring the metrics, what changed, what you learned and what you need.
Product Governance :: Standards and forums that guide what is built, approved and retired. Good governance speeds decisions by making rules clear in advance.
Managing Product Teams :: Hire, coach, set expectations and give feedback to PMs. Give feedback close to the event and focus on behaviour and outcome.
Strategic Alignment :: Make sure team goals connect to company strategy and to each other. Check alignment by asking teams to explain the company goal in their own words.
Product Culture :: Shared habits of curiosity, customer focus, evidence and accountability. Culture shows in what teams do when no one is watching, such as how they handle a failed launch.
