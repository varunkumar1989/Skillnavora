Product KPIs :: Product KPIs are a small set of measures tied to goals that show whether the product is succeeding. Pick five or fewer, and define each one precisely, including the numerator, denominator, time window and filters.
North Star Metrics :: The north star is the one measure of the value customers receive, such as completed bookings per week for a travel app. It should lead revenue and be supported by input metrics the teams can influence.
Acquisition Metrics :: Acquisition metrics show how users arrive: visitors, signups, cost per acquisition and conversion by channel. Compare channels on the quality of the users they bring, not only the volume.
Activation Metrics :: Activation measures whether new users reach their first moment of value, such as sending a first invoice. Define the activation event from data showing which early actions predict long-term retention.
Engagement Metrics :: Engagement measures how deeply and often users use the product: sessions, key actions, time in product and stickiness. Engagement should be tied to value, because time spent alone can reflect confusion.
Retention Metrics :: Retention is the share of users or revenue that remains over time. Report it by cohort and over several time periods, so changes in product quality are visible.
Revenue Metrics :: Revenue metrics include total revenue, average revenue per user, expansion and contraction, margin and bookings. Separate recurring and one-time revenue to see the health of the subscription base.
Customer Satisfaction Metrics :: These capture how customers feel about the product or an interaction. Satisfaction is a lagging signal, so pair it with behavioural metrics to see whether opinions match actions.
NPS, CSAT and CES :: NPS asks how likely people are to recommend the product, CSAT rates satisfaction with an interaction, and CES measures how much effort a task took. Use CES after support interactions and NPS for relationship health.
DAU, WAU and MAU :: Daily, weekly and monthly active users count distinct users. The DAU/MAU ratio indicates stickiness. A product used once a month needs different measures than one used every day.
ARPU and ARPPU :: ARPU is average revenue per user across all users, and ARPPU is average revenue per paying user. Comparing them shows how much of the change comes from conversion to paid versus price or upsell.
Customer Lifetime Value (LTV) :: LTV is the expected gross profit from a customer over their relationship with the product. A simple version multiplies average monthly gross profit by average lifespan in months. Use it with CAC to decide how much acquisition spending is sustainable.
Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) :: CAC is total sales and marketing spend divided by the number of new customers acquired in the same period. Track it by channel, and be clear about whether it includes sales salaries.
LTV/CAC Ratio :: LTV divided by CAC shows the return on acquisition spending. A ratio around 3 is often cited as healthy for subscription businesses, but the benchmark depends on payback period and cash position.
Monthly Recurring Revenue (MRR) :: MRR is predictable subscription revenue normalised to a month. Track its components: new, expansion, contraction and churned MRR. The movement between them explains growth or decline.
Annual Recurring Revenue (ARR) :: ARR annualises recurring revenue, typically MRR multiplied by 12. It is useful for comparing subscription businesses, but it must exclude one-time fees and be defined consistently.
Churn and Retention Rate :: Churn is the share of customers or revenue lost in a period, and retention is the share kept. They sum to 100% over the same base. Report logo churn and revenue churn separately, since large customers can distort revenue figures.
Feature Adoption :: Feature adoption is the share of eligible users who try a feature and keep using it. A launched feature used by 2% of users after three months needs investigation into discovery, value or fit.
Product Health Metrics :: A balanced set includes reliability, performance, quality, support load, satisfaction and usage. Health metrics warn of problems before growth metrics decline.
Product Performance Reviews :: A regular review reads metrics, explains changes, agrees actions and adjusts the roadmap. Each review should end with named owners and dates for the actions it creates.
