SUBJECT 01Product Management Foundations16 topics

What a product manager does, how the role differs from its neighbours, and how a product moves from idea to growth. This subject sets the vocabulary used in every later subject.

  1. Introduction to Product ManagementThe discipline of deciding what to build, for whom and why, then guiding it from idea to a product that delivers customer and business value.
  2. Evolution of Product ManagementFrom brand and packaged-goods managers, through software and agile teams, to today's data-driven and AI-assisted product teams.
  3. Product Manager Roles and ResponsibilitiesOwning the problem, vision, strategy, roadmap, priorities and outcomes, and aligning engineering, design, data, marketing and sales around them.
  4. Product Manager vs Product Owner vs Project Manager vs Business AnalystPM owns the why and what; the product owner manages the backlog in Scrum; the project manager owns schedule and delivery; the analyst owns detailed requirements and process.
  5. Product Manager vs Product Analyst vs Product OperationsThe analyst answers questions with data; product operations builds the processes, tooling and insights that let PMs work faster; the PM decides what to do.
  6. Types of Product ManagersCore, technical, growth, platform, data, AI, B2B, B2C and enterprise PMs, each weighting skills differently.
  7. Product Lifecycle ManagementManaging a product through introduction, growth, maturity and decline, adjusting strategy, investment and metrics at each stage.
  8. Product Discovery vs Product DeliveryDiscovery decides what is worth building through research and experiments; delivery builds and ships it. Strong teams run both continuously.
  9. Product Thinking and Customer-CentricityStarting from the customer's problem and the outcome they want, then working back to features, instead of starting from solutions.
  10. Product Vision, Mission and ObjectivesMission says why the company exists, vision describes the future state the product enables, and objectives set the measurable steps toward it.
  11. Product Strategy FundamentalsA coherent set of choices on where to play and how to win, backed by evidence about customers, market and capabilities.
  12. Product-Market FitThe point at which a product satisfies strong market demand, seen in retention, organic growth and customers who would be very disappointed to lose it.
  13. Product-Led GrowthA model where the product itself drives acquisition, conversion and expansion, through free tiers, trials, self-serve onboarding and viral loops.
  14. B2B, B2C, B2B2C and SaaS Product ModelsWho buys and who uses differ by model: businesses, consumers, businesses serving consumers, and subscription software delivered online.
  15. Product Management Operating ModelsHow product work is organised: feature teams, stream-aligned teams, platform teams, centralised or embedded PMs, and how they plan together.
  16. Product Management Career PathsAssociate PM to PM, senior, group, director and CPO, plus branches into specialist, founder and AI product roles.

SUBJECT 02Customer Discovery and Market Research22 topics

Methods to find real problems worth solving, understand customers deeply and size and test the opportunity before heavy investment.

  1. Design ThinkingA human-centred loop of empathise, define, ideate, prototype and test used to explore problems and solutions.
  2. Problem Identification and ValidationFinding a problem, then proving it is frequent, painful and valuable enough that people will change behaviour or pay.
  3. Customer Research MethodologiesInterviews, surveys, observation, diary studies, usability tests and data analysis, chosen to match the question.
  4. Qualitative and Quantitative ResearchQualitative research explains why people behave as they do; quantitative research measures how many and how much.
  5. Customer InterviewsOpen, non-leading conversations about past behaviour and real problems; ask for stories, not opinions about your idea.
  6. Surveys and Feedback CollectionStructured questions at scale; keep them short, unbiased and tied to a decision, and pair them with interviews.
  7. User PersonasEvidence-based archetypes of key user groups with goals, context and pain points, used to keep decisions grounded.
  8. Jobs to Be Done (JTBD)People hire products to make progress in a situation; the job, not demographics, explains what they choose.
  9. Customer Pain PointsSpecific frustrations in a workflow, ranked by severity and frequency, that products can relieve.
  10. Customer Journey MappingA map of stages, actions, thoughts and emotions from awareness to loyalty, exposing gaps and moments that matter.
  11. User Journey AnalysisExamining how users actually move through the product, using behaviour data to find drop-offs and detours.
  12. Empathy MappingA one-page view of what a user says, thinks, does and feels to align the team on their perspective.
  13. Voice of Customer (VoC)Systematically collecting and synthesising customer feedback from support, reviews, sales calls and surveys.
  14. Market ResearchGathering and analysing information about market size, trends, customers and competitors to guide decisions.
  15. Market SegmentationDividing a market into groups with shared needs or behaviours so you can target and position precisely.
  16. TAM, SAM and SOMTotal addressable market, the serviceable part you can reach with your model, and the share you can realistically obtain.
  17. Competitor AnalysisComparing competitors' offers, pricing, positioning and strengths to find gaps and differentiation.
  18. SWOT AnalysisStrengths, weaknesses, opportunities and threats, a quick structured view of internal and external factors.
  19. Porter's Five ForcesIndustry attractiveness from rivalry, new entrants, substitutes, buyer power and supplier power.
  20. Value Proposition CanvasMatches customer jobs, pains and gains to the products, pain relievers and gain creators you offer.
  21. Opportunity AssessmentA short brief that asks the key questions: problem, audience, size, alternatives, why now, success measures and risks.
  22. Hypothesis Development and ValidationTurn assumptions into testable statements with a metric and threshold, then run the cheapest test that could prove them wrong.

SUBJECT 03Product Strategy and Roadmapping20 topics

Turning vision and research into focused strategy, an outcome-based roadmap, and defensible prioritisation and investment decisions.

  1. Product Vision and Strategic AlignmentConnecting the product vision to company strategy so teams share direction and priorities.
  2. Strategic Product PlanningSetting goals, bets and sequencing over quarters and years, balancing growth, quality and capacity.
  3. Product PositioningThe place your product holds in the customer's mind relative to alternatives: for whom, what category and why better.
  4. Product DifferentiationThe distinct, valued advantage that competitors cannot easily copy, such as data, workflow depth, brand or distribution.
  5. Product Strategy FrameworksTools such as Playing to Win, Good Strategy (diagnosis, guiding policy, actions), Wardley maps and the three horizons model.
  6. Product Roadmap DevelopmentA communication tool showing problems and outcomes planned over time, flexible on detail and honest about uncertainty.
  7. Outcome-Based RoadmappingOrganising the roadmap around customer and business outcomes instead of a feature list with dates.
  8. OKRs and KPIsOKRs set ambitious objectives with measurable key results for a period; KPIs track ongoing health of the business or product.
  9. North Star MetricThe single metric that best captures the value customers get, and that leads revenue.
  10. Product Portfolio ManagementAllocating investment across multiple products or bets by stage, risk and return.
  11. Feature PrioritisationOrdering work by value, effort, risk and strategic fit with explicit criteria rather than opinion.
  12. RICE FrameworkScore = (Reach x Impact x Confidence) / Effort, a simple way to compare initiatives.
  13. MoSCoW FrameworkClassify requirements as Must, Should, Could and Won't have in this release to fix scope.
  14. Kano ModelSeparates basic expectations, performance features and delighters to see how features affect satisfaction.
  15. ICE ScoringImpact, Confidence and Ease scored 1-10 and multiplied or averaged for fast ranking.
  16. Impact vs Effort MatrixA 2x2 that shows quick wins, big bets, fill-ins and time sinks.
  17. Opportunity ScoringRates how important a customer need is against how satisfied they are; high importance with low satisfaction signals opportunity.
  18. Stakeholder PrioritisationAligning priorities with executives, sales, support and engineering using shared criteria and transparent trade-offs.
  19. Strategic Trade-OffsChoosing what not to do: speed vs quality, breadth vs depth, build now vs later, and documenting the reasoning.
  20. Product Investment DecisionsDeciding where to put people and money using expected value, strategic fit, evidence and option value.

SUBJECT 04Product Design, UX and Prototyping17 topics

The design knowledge a PM needs to partner with designers, test ideas cheaply and shape usable, accessible products, including AI-first experiences.

  1. UX FundamentalsUser experience covers the whole interaction: usefulness, usability, findability, credibility and delight.
  2. UI FundamentalsVisual interface design: layout, typography, colour, components and consistency.
  3. Human-Centred DesignDesigning with deep understanding of people and testing with them throughout.
  4. Design Thinking ProcessEmpathise, define, ideate, prototype, test, applied iteratively to product problems.
  5. Information ArchitectureOrganising and labelling content and navigation so users can find things.
  6. User FlowsDiagrams of the steps a user takes to complete a task, including decisions and error paths.
  7. WireframingLow-detail layout sketches that settle structure and content before visual design.
  8. Low-Fidelity and High-Fidelity PrototypesLow-fi (paper, boxes) tests ideas fast; high-fi looks and behaves like the real product for realistic testing.
  9. Interactive PrototypingClickable prototypes that simulate flows so you can test with users before engineering builds.
  10. Figma FundamentalsFrames, components, auto layout, variables and prototyping in the standard collaborative design tool.
  11. Usability TestingWatching representative users attempt tasks, to find where they struggle, with five users per round revealing most issues.
  12. Design ValidationConfirming a design solves the problem with evidence from tests, metrics and feedback before scaling it.
  13. Accessibility and Inclusive DesignDesigning for people with different abilities and contexts, guided by WCAG: contrast, keyboard use, screen readers and clear language.
  14. Mobile-First Product DesignDesigning for small screens and constraints first, then extending to larger screens.
  15. Conversational UXDesigning dialogue: turn-taking, clarifying questions, tone, error recovery and graceful hand-off.
  16. AI-First User ExperienceExperiences built around AI capability from the start, with uncertainty, feedback and control designed in.
  17. Human-AI Interaction DesignPatterns that set expectations, show confidence, let users correct the AI and keep them in control.

SUBJECT 05Product Requirements and Documentation20 topics

Writing clear requirements and documents that engineers can build from and teams can trace, test and release.

  1. Product Requirement Document (PRD)The shared document describing the problem, users, goals, scope, requirements, metrics and launch plan.
  2. Business Requirement Document (BRD)Business-level needs, objectives, scope and constraints, usually for stakeholders and sponsors.
  3. Functional RequirementsWhat the system must do: behaviours and features.
  4. Non-Functional RequirementsHow well it must do it: performance, security, reliability, scalability and compliance.
  5. Technical RequirementsConstraints and needs for architecture, integrations, data, platforms and infrastructure.
  6. Product SpecificationsDetailed descriptions of behaviour, rules, data and interfaces sufficient for build and test.
  7. Epics, Features and User StoriesEpics are large goals, features are deliverable capabilities, stories are small user-centred slices a team can finish in a sprint.
  8. User Story MappingArranging stories along the user journey to see the whole experience and slice releases.
  9. Acceptance CriteriaSpecific, testable conditions that must be true for a story to be accepted, often in Given/When/Then form.
  10. Definition of ReadyThe checklist a story must meet before the team starts it, such as clear value, criteria and dependencies.
  11. Definition of DoneThe shared quality bar every item must meet to count as finished: tested, reviewed, documented, deployable.
  12. Use CasesStep-by-step descriptions of how an actor achieves a goal with the system, including alternatives.
  13. Edge CasesRare or extreme inputs and states that can break flows, found by asking what happens when things are empty, huge, slow or invalid.
  14. Error ScenariosHow the product behaves when things fail, with clear messages, recovery paths and logging.
  15. Product Backlog CreationA prioritised list of everything that might be built, each item with value and clear description.
  16. Backlog RefinementRegularly clarifying, splitting, estimating and ordering backlog items so the next sprints are ready.
  17. Release DocumentationRelease notes, change logs, runbooks and support guides that explain what changed and how to operate it.
  18. Product Requirement TraceabilityLinking each requirement to its goal, design, tests and release to prove coverage and manage change.
  19. Technical DocumentationArchitecture notes, API docs and runbooks that let others build, integrate and maintain.
  20. Product Decision RecordsShort records of decisions, options considered, reasons and consequences, so context is not lost.

SUBJECT 06Agile, Scrum and Product Delivery23 topics

How teams plan, build and ship iteratively, and the tools PMs use to coordinate delivery.

  1. Agile FundamentalsIterative delivery, frequent feedback and adapting to change over following a fixed plan.
  2. Agile ManifestoFour values and twelve principles favouring individuals, working software, collaboration and responding to change.
  3. Scrum FrameworkFixed-length sprints with defined roles, events and artefacts to deliver increments.
  4. Kanban MethodologyA continuous flow board that limits work in progress and optimises throughput.
  5. Scrum Roles and ResponsibilitiesProduct Owner, Scrum Master and Developers, each accountable for a distinct part of the work.
  6. Product Owner ResponsibilitiesMaximising product value by managing and ordering the backlog and accepting work.
  7. Sprint PlanningThe team selects backlog items and plans how to deliver them within the sprint goal.
  8. Daily Stand-upsA 15-minute sync on progress toward the sprint goal and blockers.
  9. Sprint ReviewsDemonstrating the increment to stakeholders and gathering feedback.
  10. Sprint RetrospectivesThe team reflects on how it worked and agrees small improvements.
  11. Backlog GroomingOlder term for refinement: keeping the backlog clear, sized and ordered.
  12. Story PointsA relative unit of effort and complexity for sizing stories, not hours.
  13. Estimation TechniquesPlanning poker, t-shirt sizing, affinity estimation and reference-class estimates to forecast work.
  14. Velocity and Burndown ChartsVelocity is points completed per sprint; burndown shows remaining work over time. Use for forecasting, not for judging people.
  15. Release PlanningSequencing sprints into releases with dates, scope, dependencies and risk buffers; staged rollout and rollback plans belong here.
  16. Dependency ManagementIdentifying and tracking work that relies on other teams, systems or vendors, and resolving early.
  17. Risk and Issue ManagementRisks are possible problems to mitigate; issues are problems that have occurred and need action.
  18. Cross-Functional Team CollaborationProduct, design, engineering, data, QA and go-to-market working toward one goal with shared context.
  19. Engineering CollaborationWorking with engineers on trade-offs, feasibility and scope, treating them as partners from discovery onward.
  20. Product Delivery LifecycleIdea to discovery, design, build, test, release, measure and iterate.
  21. JiraIssue and sprint tracking tool for backlogs, boards, workflows and reports.
  22. ConfluenceTeam wiki used for specs, decision records and knowledge sharing, integrated with Jira.
  23. Linear and Azure DevOpsLinear is a fast, opinionated issue tracker; Azure DevOps combines boards, repos and pipelines for engineering teams.

  9. Data VisualisationChoosing charts that make comparisons obvious, with clear labels and no distortion.

  11. Product Analytics FundamentalsTracking user behaviour as events with properties, then analysing funnels, cohorts and paths.
  12. Funnel AnalysisMeasuring conversion between steps to find where users drop off.
  13. Cohort AnalysisGrouping users by a shared start (such as signup week) and following them over time.
  14. Retention AnalysisHow many users return over days or weeks; the retention curve flattening signals product-market fit.
  15. Churn AnalysisWhy and when customers leave, using exit surveys, usage patterns and segment comparison.
  16. Conversion Rate OptimisationImproving the share of visitors who complete a goal through research, tests and friction removal.
  17. Customer SegmentationGrouping users by behaviour, value or needs to tailor products and messages.
  18. A/B TestingRandomly split users between versions and compare a pre-chosen metric to measure causal impact.
  19. Multivariate TestingTesting combinations of multiple elements at once; needs more traffic than A/B.
  20. Experimentation FrameworksA repeatable process: hypothesis, metric, sample size, guardrails, run length, analysis and decision.
  21. Event TrackingDefining and logging user actions with a consistent naming plan so analysis is trustworthy.
  22. Product Analytics DashboardsLive views of key metrics for the team, with a clear owner, definitions and thresholds for action.
  23. MixpanelEvent-based product analytics for funnels, retention and cohorts.
  24. AmplitudeProduct analytics platform with behavioural cohorts, journeys and experimentation.
  25. Google AnalyticsWeb and app analytics for traffic, acquisition and conversion.
  26. Data-Driven Product DecisionsCombining quantitative data with qualitative insight, stating the decision rule in advance.

SUBJECT 08Product Metrics and Business Analytics20 topics

The metrics that describe product health and business performance, and how to read and review them.

  1. Product KPIsA small set of measures tied to goals that show whether the product is succeeding.
  2. North Star MetricsThe one measure of delivered customer value that guides the whole team, supported by input metrics.
  3. Acquisition MetricsHow users arrive: visitors, signups, cost per acquisition and channel conversion.
  4. Activation MetricsWhether new users reach the first moment of value, such as completing a key action.
  5. Engagement MetricsHow deeply and often users use the product: sessions, actions, time in product, stickiness.
  6. Retention MetricsThe share of users or revenue that remains over time.
  7. Revenue MetricsRevenue, ARPU, expansion and contraction, margin and bookings.
  8. Customer Satisfaction MetricsDirect measures of how customers feel about the product or interaction.
  9. NPS, CSAT and CESNPS asks likelihood to recommend; CSAT rates satisfaction with an interaction; CES measures how easy a task was.
  10. DAU, WAU and MAUDaily, weekly and monthly active users; DAU/MAU indicates stickiness.
  11. ARPU and ARPPUAverage revenue per user and per paying user.
  12. Customer Lifetime Value (LTV)Expected gross profit from a customer over their lifetime, from average revenue, margin and lifespan.
  13. Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC)Total sales and marketing spend divided by customers acquired in the period.
  14. LTV/CAC RatioReturn on acquisition spend; around 3 or higher is often a healthy target for subscription businesses.
  15. Monthly Recurring Revenue (MRR)Predictable subscription revenue normalised to a month.
  16. Annual Recurring Revenue (ARR)Predictable subscription revenue annualised, typically MRR x 12.
  17. Churn and Retention RateChurn is the share lost in a period; retention is the share kept, and the two sum to one.
  18. Feature AdoptionThe share of eligible users who try and keep using a feature.
  19. Product Health MetricsA balanced set: reliability, performance, quality, support load, satisfaction and usage.
  20. Product Performance ReviewsA regular forum to read metrics, explain changes, agree actions and adjust the roadmap.

SUBJECT 09Business, Finance and Product Commercialisation24 topics

How products make money and how a PM builds a financial case, sets price and takes the product to market.

  1. Business FundamentalsRevenue, cost, profit, cash flow, margins and how value flows through a company.
  2. Business Model CanvasNine blocks describing customers, value, channels, revenue, resources, activities, partners and costs.
  3. Revenue ModelsSubscription, transaction fee, advertising, licensing, usage-based, marketplace commission and services.
  4. Subscription Business ModelsRecurring fees for continued access, optimising retention, expansion and predictable revenue.
  5. Freemium ModelsA free tier drives adoption and conversion to paid; works when marginal cost is low and upgrade triggers are clear.
  6. Usage-Based PricingCustomers pay according to consumption, aligning price with value but making revenue less predictable.
  7. Product Pricing StrategySetting price using cost, competitors and value to the customer, with packaging and tiers that fit segments.
  8. Monetisation StrategiesThe mechanisms by which a product earns revenue and when in the user journey they appear.
  9. Financial ModellingA spreadsheet model of revenue, costs and cash under stated assumptions, with scenarios.
  10. Unit EconomicsProfit or loss per unit (customer or transaction) after variable costs, such as CAC, LTV and payback period.
  11. ROI and ROEReturn on investment is gain relative to cost; return on equity is net income relative to shareholder equity.
  12. Cost-Benefit AnalysisComparing total expected costs and benefits, in money terms, to decide.
  13. Break-Even AnalysisThe volume at which revenue equals total cost; fixed cost divided by contribution margin per unit.
  14. Profitability AnalysisAssessing margins by product, segment or channel to see where money is made.
  15. Business Case DevelopmentA structured argument for an investment: problem, options, costs, benefits, risks and recommendation.
  16. Budget PlanningAllocating funds to teams and initiatives, tracking against plan and forecasting.
  17. Go-to-Market StrategyWho you sell to, with what message and price, through which channels, and how you measure launch.
  18. Product Launch StrategyA staged plan for readiness, beta, announcement, enablement and post-launch monitoring.
  19. Product AdoptionMoving customers from awareness to trial to regular use through onboarding and value moments.
  20. Customer AcquisitionChannels and tactics to attract customers efficiently: content, paid, partners, sales and referrals.
  21. Customer Retention StrategyKeeping customers through onboarding, ongoing value, support, community and proactive outreach.
  22. Product Growth StrategyLoops and levers across acquisition, activation, retention, revenue and referral.
  23. Product-Led SalesUsing product usage signals to identify and convert qualified accounts, blending self-serve with sales.
  24. Enterprise Product CommercialisationSelling to large organisations: security reviews, procurement, pilots, integration and long sales cycles.

  21. System Design FundamentalsChoosing components, data flow and trade-offs to meet requirements for scale, cost and reliability.

  24. Cloud Computing FundamentalsOn-demand computing, storage and services paid by use: IaaS, PaaS, SaaS, regions and shared responsibility.
  25. AWS FundamentalsAmazon's cloud: EC2, S3, Lambda, RDS and IAM as core building blocks.
  26. Microsoft Azure FundamentalsMicrosoft's cloud: virtual machines, storage, Azure AD, Azure OpenAI and DevOps.

  28. Cybersecurity FundamentalsConfidentiality, integrity, availability, encryption, secure coding, access control and common attacks.

SUBJECT 11Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning Foundations32 topics

How AI and machine learning work, the main problem types and how models are trained and judged.

  1. Introduction to Artificial IntelligenceSystems that perform tasks associated with human intelligence, such as perception, language and decision making.
  2. History and Evolution of AIRules and symbolic AI, expert systems, statistical ML, deep learning from 2012, transformers from 2017, and generative AI.
  3. Traditional AI vs Generative AITraditional models classify or predict from data; generative models create new text, images, audio or code.
  4. Narrow AI vs General AINarrow AI does specific tasks; general AI would match human breadth, and does not yet exist.
  5. AI Applications Across IndustriesFraud detection, recommendations, diagnostics, document processing, support automation, forecasting and more.
  6. Machine Learning FundamentalsAlgorithms that learn patterns from data to predict or decide without explicit rules.
  7. Supervised LearningLearning from labelled examples to predict labels for new data.
  8. Unsupervised LearningFinding structure in unlabelled data, such as clusters or anomalies.
  9. Semi-Supervised LearningUsing a small labelled set plus a large unlabelled set.
  10. Reinforcement LearningAn agent learns by trial and error to maximise cumulative reward.
  11. ClassificationPredicting a category, such as spam or not spam.
  12. RegressionPredicting a continuous number, such as price or demand.
  13. ClusteringGrouping similar items without labels, used for segmentation.
  14. Recommendation SystemsSuggesting items using collaborative filtering, content features or hybrid models.
  15. Predictive AnalyticsUsing historical data to forecast outcomes such as churn or demand.
  16. Feature EngineeringCreating informative inputs from raw data to help models learn.
  17. Training, Validation and TestingTrain to fit, validate to tune, and test once on unseen data for an honest estimate.
  18. Model Training and InferenceTraining learns parameters from data; inference uses the trained model to make predictions.
  19. Overfitting and UnderfittingOverfitting memorises training noise and fails on new data; underfitting is too simple to capture the pattern.
  20. Bias-Variance Trade-OffSimple models have high bias; complex models have high variance; aim for the balance with best generalisation.
  21. Model Performance MetricsAccuracy, precision, recall, F1, AUC for classification; MAE and RMSE for regression, picked to match business cost of errors.
  22. Precision, Recall and F1 ScorePrecision is correctness of positive predictions; recall is coverage of actual positives; F1 balances both.
  23. Confusion MatrixA table of true/false positives and negatives that shows the types of errors.
  24. ROC-AUCArea under the curve of true-positive vs false-positive rate across thresholds; 0.5 is chance, 1.0 is perfect.
  25. Deep Learning FundamentalsMulti-layer neural networks that learn features automatically from large data.
  26. Neural NetworksLayers of connected units with weights, trained by backpropagation and gradient descent.
  27. CNN, RNN and LSTMCNNs process images, RNNs process sequences, LSTMs handle longer dependencies in sequences.
  28. TransformersAn architecture based on attention that processes sequences in parallel and underlies modern language models.
  29. Attention MechanismsA way for a model to weigh which parts of the input matter for each output.
  30. Natural Language ProcessingTechniques for understanding and generating human language, from tokenisation to LLMs.
  31. Computer Vision FundamentalsTechniques for classifying, detecting and segmenting content in images and video.
  32. AI Model LifecycleProblem framing, data, training, evaluation, deployment, monitoring and retraining.
