BRD vs FRD vs PRD

Business analysts usually write the BRD first (why and what the business needs), then the FRD (what the system must do). Product managers write PRDs.

BRDFRDPRD
PurposeWhy the business needs it: problem, goals, scope, valueWhat the system must do: functions, rules, data, screensWhat the product is and why users need it: problem, users, stories, metrics
Written byBusiness analyst with business ownersBusiness analyst / systems analyst with tech leadsProduct manager
AudienceSponsors, leadership, business teamsDevelopers, testers, architectsEngineering, design, business, leadership
LevelHigh level, business languageDetailed, testable, system languageMedium: user-focused with requirements
Key contentsObjectives, stakeholders, current vs future state, business requirements, KPIs, ROI, constraintsUse cases, functional requirements, business rules, data, integrations, UI, acceptance criteria, traceabilityProblem, goals, personas, stories, requirements, metrics, launch
Common inBanks, insurance, IT services, ERP/CRM projects, enterprisesSame projects, after the BRD is approvedProduct companies and startups

BRD template

SectionWhat to write
1. Document controlVersion, author, reviewers, approvers, sign-off dates.
2. Executive summaryThe problem, the proposed solution and expected value in 5–6 lines.
3. Business objectivesSMART objectives, e.g. 'Cut loan approval time from 5 days to 1 day by Q4'.
4. Background and current state (AS-IS)How the process works today, pain points, data, process map.
5. Future state (TO-BE)How it will work after the change, with a process map.
6. ScopeIn scope, out of scope, assumptions, constraints.
7. StakeholdersStakeholder list and RACI (Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, Informed).
8. Business requirementsBR-IDs with description, priority (MoSCoW), owner and rationale.
9. KPIs and success criteriaBaseline, target, how and when measured.
10. Cost-benefit and ROICosts, benefits, payback period; options considered.
11. Risks, dependencies and complianceRisks with mitigations; RBI/SEBI/IRDAI/DPDP or other rules.
12. ApprovalSign-off by sponsor and business owners.

FRD template

SectionWhat to write
1. IntroductionPurpose, scope, references to the approved BRD, glossary.
2. System overviewContext diagram, users and roles, systems involved.
3. Use cases / user storiesActor, preconditions, main flow, alternate flows, exceptions, postconditions.
4. Functional requirementsFR-IDs: 'The system shall…', priority, linked BR-ID, acceptance criteria (Given / When / Then).
5. Business rulesCalculations, validations, eligibility rules, decision tables.
6. Data requirementsData dictionary: field, type, length, mandatory, source, validation; entity diagram.
7. Interfaces and integrationsAPIs, file formats, third-party systems, frequency, error handling.
8. UI requirementsScreens, wireframes, field behaviour, messages.
9. Reports and dashboardsReport name, users, filters, metrics, refresh frequency.
10. Non-functional requirementsPerformance, availability, security, audit, accessibility, data retention.
11. Traceability matrix (RTM)BR-ID → FR-ID → test case ID, so nothing is missed.
12. Assumptions, open issues and sign-offOpen items with owners; sign-off by business and tech.

Business analysis techniques to use

  • Stakeholder interviews & workshops
    Elicit needs; record decisions and owners.
  • AS-IS / TO-BE process maps (BPMN)
    Draw today's and future process with swimlanes.
  • MoSCoW prioritisation
    Must, Should, Could, Won't for every requirement.
  • User stories + Gherkin acceptance criteria
    Given / When / Then makes requirements testable.
  • Use-case diagrams and UML
    Actors and system boundaries at a glance.
  • Decision tables
    Capture complex rules, e.g. loan eligibility by income and score.
  • Data dictionary & ER diagram
    Define every field once, for developers and analysts.
  • Requirements Traceability Matrix
    Link business need → requirement → test case.
  • Gap analysis & root cause (5 Whys, fishbone)
    Find what to fix before writing requirements.
  • Wireframes & prototypes
    Show screens early to avoid rework.
  • SWOT, PESTLE, cost-benefit
    Build the business case in the BRD.
  • UAT planning
    Test scenarios that prove business requirements are met.
