The PRD template, section by section

What each section should answer, with a worked example (SplitKaro, a UPI bill-split app).

SectionWhat to writeExample
1. OverviewProduct name, owner, status, last updated, one-line summary and links (designs, research, tickets).SplitKaro v1 · Owner: PM · Status: Draft · Split and settle group expenses in one tap through UPI.
2. Problem statementWho has the problem, how often, how painful, evidence (data, interviews), why now.62% of 40 interviewed students track shared spends on WhatsApp; 1 in 3 groups never fully settles.
3. Goals and non-goalsBusiness and user goals with targets; what you will NOT do in this release.Goal: 50k MAU in 6 months. Non-goal: lending or credit in v1.
4. Users and personas2–3 personas with context, goals and pain points; jobs-to-be-done.Riya, 20, hostel student: 'When we order food together, I want to split instantly so no one feels awkward.'
5. User journeys and storiesKey flows and user stories with priority (P0/P1/P2) and acceptance criteria.P0: As a group member, I want to add an expense in 3 taps so that everyone sees what they owe.
6. RequirementsFunctional requirements (ID, description, priority, acceptance criteria) and non-functional (performance, security, privacy, accessibility).F-04: Settle via UPI intent; AC: payment status updates within 5 seconds.
7. Design and UXWireframes or Figma links, key screens, edge cases, empty and error states.Empty state: 'Add your first expense' with a sample group.
8. AI / data (if any)Model or rules, data needed, evaluation, guardrails, human fallback, cost per request.Receipt OCR: 95% amount accuracy on a 500-receipt test set; manual edit always allowed.
9. Success metricsNorth Star, input metrics, guardrail metrics with baseline and target.North Star: groups settled in 7 days. Guardrail: payment failure rate < 1%.
10. Launch planMVP scope, phases, experiment design, rollout %, marketing and support readiness.Beta in 3 colleges → 10% rollout → full launch; A/B test reminder timing.
11. Risks and dependenciesTechnical, legal, operational risks with mitigations; teams and vendors you depend on.Dependency: UPI partner bank. Risk: DPDP consent for contacts; mitigation: explicit opt-in.
12. Open questions and timelineDecisions still needed, owners, dates; milestones.Q: Charge a convenience fee? Owner: PM, decide by sprint 3.

How to frame a PRD like top PMs

Use this order of thinking before you write a single requirement.

  • 1. Problem before solution. Who is hurting, how often, how do you know? Add data or quotes.
  • 2. One clear goal. A North Star metric with baseline and target, plus guardrails.
  • 3. Say what you won't do. Non-goals stop scope creep.
  • 4. Stories with acceptance criteria. "As a…, I want…, so that…" + Given / When / Then.
  • 5. Edge cases and states. Empty, error, offline, slow network, accessibility.
  • 6. Measure and launch. Experiment design, rollout %, what success looks like in 30/60/90 days.
  • 7. Risks and open questions. Each with an owner and a date.
  • 8. Keep it short and alive. 2–5 pages, link designs and research, update as decisions change.

Common PRD mistakes

  • ❌ Starting with the solution instead of the user problem and evidence.
  • ❌ No measurable goals: 'improve engagement' instead of 'raise D30 retention from 18% to 25%'.
  • ❌ Missing non-goals, so scope keeps growing.
  • ❌ User stories without acceptance criteria, so engineers and QA guess.
  • ❌ Ignoring edge cases, error states, accessibility and privacy (DPDP).
  • ❌ Writing a 40-page document nobody reads: keep it skimmable and link details.
  • ❌ Not updating the PRD after decisions change; it should be a living document.
  • ❌ No owner or date for open questions.
