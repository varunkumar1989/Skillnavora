Module 1 CRM and Digital Marketing Fundamentals Beginner

What CRM and digital marketing are, the customer lifecycle, channels and the metrics every campaign is judged on.

1.1 CRM and the customer lifecycle

What is it?

A CRM (customer relationship management) system stores customers, their interactions and their history. The lifecycle runs from first awareness to acquisition, onboarding, engagement, retention and re-engagement.

Why is it important?

Marketing automation only works when the customer data and lifecycle stage are right. Most campaign mistakes come from wrong audiences, not wrong emails.

How does it work and the steps

  1. List the lifecycle stages for your product (for example: visitor, lead, customer, active, lapsed).
  2. For each stage, write one message and one goal.
  3. Map each stage to the data you need (sign-up date, last purchase, last open).

Real-world example

A bank: a visitor who applies for a card moves to lead; approval makes them a customer; no transaction for 90 days starts a re-engagement journey.

Common errors

Mixing up lead and customer, so a paying customer gets an acquisition offer.

Interview question

Explain the customer lifecycle and one message for each stage.

Hands-on exercise

1.2 Channels and marketing metrics

What is it?

Digital channels include email, SMS, push notifications, web and social media, plus paid advertising. Key metrics: open rate, click-through rate, click-to-open rate, conversion rate, bounce rate, unsubscribe rate and revenue per send.

Why is it important?

Metrics show whether a campaign works and where to fix it. Open rates are affected by Apple Mail privacy features, so click-based metrics are often more reliable.

How does it work and the steps

  1. Calculate open rate as unique opens divided by delivered emails.
  2. Calculate click-through rate as unique clicks divided by delivered emails.
  3. Calculate click-to-open rate as unique clicks divided by unique opens.

Real-world example

1,000 delivered, 250 unique opens, 40 unique clicks: open rate 25%, CTR 4%, CTOR 16%.

Common errors

Comparing open rates between campaigns that used different send times or audiences.

Interview question

What is the difference between CTR and CTOR?

Hands-on exercise

2.1 The Salesforce clouds

What is it?

Salesforce sells several clouds: Sales Cloud (sales), Service Cloud (support), Marketing Cloud (marketing), Experience Cloud (portals), Data Cloud (unified customer data), Commerce Cloud (e-commerce) and the Salesforce Platform used to build apps.

Why is it important?

Projects often connect Marketing Cloud to Sales or Service Cloud data. Knowing each cloud's job tells you where the data should come from.

How does it work and the steps

  1. Name the cloud that owns the customer's order history, support tickets and marketing consent in a typical company.
  2. Decide which cloud is the system of record for each.

Real-world example

A retailer keeps orders in Commerce Cloud, support cases in Service Cloud, and sends campaigns from Marketing Cloud.

What is it?

Marketing Cloud Engagement is the product for email, mobile, journeys and automation. Other Salesforce marketing products include Marketing Cloud Personalization, Marketing Cloud Intelligence and editions such as Growth and Advanced. Feature availability depends on the edition and the licence on your account.

A proposal that needs real-time web personalisation must confirm the Personalization product is licensed, not assume Engagement includes it.

3.1 Business units, the MID and account structure

What is it?

An SFMC account can hold an enterprise account with a parent business unit and child business units. Each business unit has its own MID (Marketing Cloud ID) and separate sending, data and assets, unless shared.

Why is it important?

The business unit design controls who sees which data and which sender domain is used. It is hard to change later, so design it first.

How does it work and the steps

  1. List the brands or countries that need separate sending and data.
  2. Create one child business unit per brand or country that needs isolation.
  3. Decide which data extensions and assets are shared at parent level.

Real-world example

A group with two brands uses two child business units, each with its own sender profile and subscriber lists.

Common errors

Creating too many business units, which splits data and makes reporting harder.

3.2 Users, roles, sender authentication and delivery settings

What is it?

Users get roles and permission sets that decide what they can do in each business unit. Sender authentication (SPF, DKIM, and a branded tracking domain where used) proves to mailbox providers that a sender is legitimate. Sender and delivery profiles hold the From name, address and reply settings.

Why is it important?

Poor authentication causes emails to land in spam and damages the sending reputation of the whole account.

How does it work and the steps

  1. Create users with the least permissions they need.
  2. Set up sender authentication with the DNS records provided by SFMC.
  3. Create a sender profile and a delivery profile for each brand.

Real-world example

Adding the DKIM record in DNS and checking it in SFMC before the first campaign.

Common errors

Sending from a domain you have not authenticated.

4.1 Subscribers, contacts and keys

What is it?

A subscriber is a person in Email Studio, identified by a subscriber key. A contact is a person in Contact Builder, identified by a contact key, and one contact can own several subscribers (for example, one per email address or business unit). Subscriber keys must be unique and stable.

Why is it important?

Wrong keys are the most common cause of duplicate emails and lost unsubscribes.

How does it work and the steps

  1. Choose a stable identifier (customer ID) as the subscriber key.
  2. Never use the email address as the key if the address can change.
  3. Check that the contact key matches the subscriber key in your model.

Real-world example

Customer 10045 changes email address; the subscriber key stays 10045, so history and unsubscribes still apply.

Common errors

Using email address as the key, then creating a new subscriber each time the address changes.

Interview question

What is the difference between a subscriber key and a contact key?

Hands-on exercise

List three possible subscriber key choices for a retailer and pick one with a reason.

4.2 Data architecture: keys, relationships and data views

What is it?

Relational data uses primary keys (unique identifiers) and foreign keys (links to other tables). In SFMC, data extensions are the tables; relationships are modelled with keys and in Contact Builder.

Why is it important?

A clear model avoids joins that multiply rows and produce wrong counts.

How does it work and the steps

  1. Define the primary key for each data extension.
  2. Define the foreign key that links orders to customers.
  3. Draw the relationship diagram before building.

Real-world example

Customers (CustomerID primary) and Orders (OrderID primary, CustomerID foreign).

Common errors

Joining on a non-unique field, which multiplies rows.

Interview question

5.1 Creating and configuring data extensions

What is it?

A data extension is an SFMC table. Types: standard, sendable (linked to a subscriber key and used for sends), non-sendable, and test. Each has fields with types, a primary key, nullable settings and retention settings.

Why is it important?

Choosing the right type and key decides whether the data can be used for sending and how long it stays.

How does it work and the steps

  1. Create a data extension from the Email Studio or Contact Builder navigation.
  2. Add fields with the right type (Text, Number, Date, Email, Phone, Boolean).
  3. Set the primary key and make key fields non-nullable.
  4. Set retention, for example delete all records after 365 days.

Real-world example

A sendable data extension for customers with SubscriberKey as the send relationship field.

Common errors

Choosing text for dates, so date filters fail.

Interview question

What is a sendable data extension?

Hands-on exercise

5.2 Importing, updating and cleaning data

What is it?

Data can be imported from CSV files, SFTP, Salesforce CRM, APIs and databases. Import options include overwrite, append, and update or insert on the primary key. Data quality checks catch duplicates, bad dates and missing keys.

Why is it important?

Bad imports send emails to the wrong people. Validate before loading.

How does it work and the steps

  1. Prepare the CSV with a header row matching the field names.
  2. Use the import activity with the update-or-insert option on the primary key.
  3. Check row counts before and after the import.
  4. Remove duplicates with a SQL query before the send.

Real-world example

Import 10,000 rows; the import reports 9,980 rows, so 20 rows had duplicate keys.

Code example

-- Find duplicate keys before a send
SELECT SubscriberKey, COUNT(*) AS Copies
FROM Customers
GROUP BY SubscriberKey
HAVING COUNT(*) > 1;

Common errors

Choosing overwrite when append was intended, and losing history.

Interview question

How would you import daily customer updates without creating duplicates?

6.1 SQL fundamentals in SFMC

What is it?

SFMC SQL runs in Query Activities inside Automation Studio. It uses a T-SQL style syntax with some restrictions compared with SQL Server. Core clauses: SELECT, FROM, WHERE, GROUP BY, HAVING, ORDER BY.

Why is it important?

Audiences, reports and suppression lists are almost always built with SQL in SFMC.

How does it work and the steps

  1. Write the query in the Query Activity.
  2. Set a target data extension and an update option (overwrite or append).
  3. Run the activity, then check the row count in the target.

Real-world example

Select active customers from the Customers data extension into an Audience data extension.

Common errors

Writing a query that works in SQL Server but is not supported in SFMC. Always test in the Query Activity.

Interview question

What is a Query Activity?

Hands-on exercise

Write a query that selects customers in one city and counts them.

6.2 Aggregation, joins and CTEs

What is it?

Aggregates (COUNT, SUM, AVG, MIN, MAX) with GROUP BY summarise data. JOIN combines data extensions and system data views. Common table expressions (WITH) name steps. Check the official SQL restrictions for the exact features your account supports.

Why is it important?

Joins and aggregates produce segments and reports; a wrong join silently changes audience size.

How does it work and the steps

  1. Use INNER JOIN when both sides must match.
  2. Use LEFT JOIN to keep all customers and show missing orders as NULL.
  3. Count distinct customers, not rows, when a customer has many orders.

Real-world example

Customers with no order in 90 days: LEFT JOIN orders and keep rows where the order is NULL.

Code example

-- Customers with no order in the last 90 days
SELECT c.SubscriberKey, c.EmailAddress
FROM Customers c
LEFT JOIN Orders o
  ON o.SubscriberKey = c.SubscriberKey
  AND o.OrderDate >= DATEADD(day, -90, GETDATE())
WHERE o.OrderID IS NULL;
-- Line by line: LEFT JOIN keeps every customer; the date condition is inside the join so only recent orders match;
-- WHERE o.OrderID IS NULL keeps customers with no matching recent order.

Common errors

Counting rows where you need distinct customers.

Interview question

Explain the difference between INNER JOIN and LEFT JOIN.

Hands-on exercise

6.3 SFMC data views

What is it?

System data views hold sends, opens, clicks, bounces, unsubscribes, jobs, journey activity and SMS tracking. Examples: _Subscribers, _Sent, _Open, _Click, _Bounce, _Unsubscribe, _Job, _Journey, _JourneyActivity, _SMSMessageTracking, _Complaint, _BusinessUnitUnsubscribes. Each has its own keys and retention limit.

Why is it important?

Engagement analysis and suppression depend on these views. Retention limits mean old data may be gone, so keep your own history.

How does it work and the steps

  1. Check the view's documentation for keys and retention.
  2. Join on SubscriberKey and JobID where the view supports them.
  3. Export long-term history to your own data extension.

Real-world example

Last-open date per subscriber: SELECT SubscriberKey, MAX(EventDate) FROM _Open GROUP BY SubscriberKey.

Common errors

Assuming a data view keeps data forever.

Interview question

What is _Open used for and what limits apply?

7.1 Sends, classifications and subscriber status

What is it?

An email send is a definition with a data source, a send classification (commercial or transactional, depending on the law and consent), a sender profile and a delivery profile. Subscriber status includes active, unsubscribed, bounced and held.

Why is it important?

Sending commercial email to unsubscribed people breaks the law in many places and damages reputation.

How does it work and the steps

  1. Create the email, then the send definition.
  2. Choose the send classification that matches the message (commercial needs consent and an unsubscribe link).
  3. Use suppression lists for people who must not receive the message.

Real-world example

A password reset is transactional; a promotion is commercial with unsubscribe.

Common errors

Using a commercial classification for transactional mail, or the reverse.

Interview question

7.2 Deliverability: SPF, DKIM, DMARC and bounce management

What is it?

SPF lists the servers allowed to send for a domain; DKIM signs messages; DMARC tells receivers what to do when checks fail. Bounces are hard (invalid address) or soft (temporary). Complaints and bounce rates affect reputation.

Why is it important?

Good deliverability decides whether the message is seen at all.

How does it work and the steps

  1. Publish SPF and DKIM for the sending domain.
  2. Add a DMARC policy starting with monitoring (p=none).
  3. Remove hard-bounced addresses from the send.
  4. Watch complaint rates after each send.

Real-world example

A hard bounce is suppressed automatically; a soft bounce is retried by the system.

Common errors

Sending to old lists without removing bounces.

Interview question

What does DMARC do?

8.2 Responsive HTML email

What is it?

Email HTML uses table-based layouts, inline CSS and media queries for mobile. Email clients have different support, so test in several clients.

Why is it important?

Most emails are opened on phones; a layout that breaks on mobile loses clicks.

How does it work and the steps

  1. Use a single-column table layout with a maximum width of 600px.
  2. Inline the key styles.
  3. Use alt text on images and a text fallback for buttons.
  4. Test on mobile and desktop clients.

Real-world example

A two-column layout collapses to one column below 480px using a media query.

Code example

<!DOCTYPE html>
<html>
<body style="margin:0;background:#f4f4f7;font-family:Arial,sans-serif">
<table role="presentation" width="100%" cellpadding="0" cellspacing="0"><tr><td align="center">
  <table role="presentation" width="600" style="max-width:600px;background:#ffffff">
    <tr><td style="padding:24px;text-align:center;font-size:22px;font-weight:bold;color:#2b1a66">Your new offers</td></tr>
    <tr><td style="padding:0 24px 24px;font-size:16px;color:#1d1b2e">
      Hi %%=ProperCase(AttributeValue("FirstName"))=%%, here are picks for you.
    </td></tr>
    <tr><td align="center" style="padding:0 24px 32px">

    </td></tr>
  </table>
</td></tr></table>
</body>
</html>

Relying on CSS features that many clients ignore.

9.1 Syntax, variables and personalisation

What is it?

AMPscript runs on the server inside %%[ ... ]%% blocks and prints values with %%=...=%%. SET assigns a variable; AttributeValue reads a subscriber attribute; ProperCase formats names.

Why is it important?

Almost every personalised SFMC email or CloudPage uses AMPscript.

How does it work and the steps

  1. Declare with VAR and assign with SET.
  2. Print with %%= ... =%%.
  3. Use AttributeValue for subscriber fields and provide a default in IIF or a check.

Real-world example

Hello %%=ProperCase(AttributeValue("FirstName"))=%%.

Code example

%%[
  VAR @first, @greeting
  SET @first = AttributeValue("FirstName")
  IF Empty(@first) THEN
    SET @greeting = "there"
  ELSE
    SET @greeting = ProperCase(@first)
  ENDIF
]%%
Hello %%=v(@greeting)=%%,
// Line by line: VAR declares the variables; SET reads the subscriber's first name;
// IF/ELSE chooses the fallback when the field is empty; v() prints the variable.

Common errors

Printing an empty first name, which shows 'Hello ,'.

Interview question

What does AttributeValue return if the attribute is missing?

Hands-on exercise

9.2 Data extension lookups

What is it?

Lookup returns one value from a data extension by matching a field. LookupRows returns a set of rows. RowCount counts them. Always handle the case when nothing is found.

Why is it important?

Lookups personalise messages from other data (orders, loyalty points) without a separate send.

How does it work and the steps

  1. Use Lookup('DataExtension','ReturnField','MatchField',value).
  2. Check RowCount on LookupRows before looping.
  3. Use the subscriber key or email address as the match value.

Real-world example

Show the loyalty points for the subscriber's email.

Code example

%%[
  VAR @tier
  SET @tier = Lookup("LoyaltyMembers", "Tier", "EmailAddress", emailaddr)
  IF Empty(@tier) THEN SET @tier = "Member" ENDIF
]%%
Your tier: %%=v(@tier)=%%
// Lookup matches EmailAddress to the subscriber email (emailaddr); if no row, @tier is empty and the default is used.

Common errors

Lookup on a field that is not unique and returns a random row.

Interview question

Explain Lookup and LookupRows.

Hands-on exercise

12.2 Monitoring, errors and retries

What is it?

Automation status shows which activity failed. Verification activities check files or row counts before the next step. Failure notifications and logs help support teams.

Why is it important?

18.1 REST authentication with an installed package

What is it?

An installed package in SFMC gives an app a client ID and secret. The app requests an access token from the account's auth endpoint, then calls REST endpoints with the token. Token requests use your account subdomain.

Why is it important?

Every integration starts with authentication; mistakes cause 401 errors and leaked credentials.

How does it work and the steps

  1. Create an installed package with the required API permissions.
  2. Copy the client ID, secret and subdomain (store them in a secrets manager).
  3. Request a token and call one endpoint.

Real-world example

POST to the token endpoint, then GET a data extension list with the bearer token.

Code example

# Token request (replace the subdomain and credentials; never commit them)
curl -X POST "https://YOUR_SUBDOMAIN.auth.marketingcloudapis.com/v2/token" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{"grant_type":"client_credentials","client_id":"YOUR_ID","client_secret":"YOUR_SECRET"}'
# The response contains access_token. Use it as: Authorization: Bearer <access_token>

Common errors

Putting the client secret in front-end code.

Interview question

Explain how OAuth works in an SFMC integration.

Hands-on exercise

Authenticate with a sandbox account and list data extensions with Postman.

18.2 REST versus SOAP and common patterns

What is it?

REST uses JSON over HTTP and is the recommended approach for new work. SOAP uses XML and WSDL and remains available for some legacy operations. Patterns: create triggered sends, upsert contacts, read tracking data, manage assets.

Why is it important?

Choosing the right API avoids rework; REST is simpler to test.

How does it work and the steps

  1. Use REST for new integrations.
  2. Check the docs for each operation's rate limits.
  3. Log every request and response status.

Real-world example

Triggered send: POST with the subscriber, the email definition and personalisation attributes.

Common errors

Sending bulk data one record at a time instead of using batch endpoints.
