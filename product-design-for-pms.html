Product Design for PMs: Complete Guide

How great products are designed and how PMs lead that work: research, process, UX principles, flows, visual systems, testing, accessibility, e-commerce and fintech use cases, behaviour design, metrics and interview prep.

In one line

Product design is deciding how a product works and feels so real people can solve a real problem easily. PMs do not draw screens; they own the problem, the users and the outcomes, and they work with designers to get there.

The 18 classes (click a class to read it in full)

Basic · Class 1

What product design is, and what a PM owns

Understand the craft, the team and the PM's role.

  • Product design covers the whole experience, not only looks.
  • Five planes: strategy, scope, structure, skeleton, surface.
  • PM owns the problem and outcomes; designer owns the solution craft; both own the user.

Know your users: research methods and when to use them

Choose the right way to learn from users.

  • Mix qualitative (why) and quantitative (how many).
  • JTBD and journey maps turn research into design direction.
  • Test small and often.

The product design process, step by step

Follow a repeatable path from problem to launch.

  • Design is a loop, not a handoff.
  • Define the problem and metric before screens.
  • Compare at least three options.

Core UX principles every PM must know

Learn the rules of thumb that predict good and bad design.

  • Heuristics are a fast review checklist.
  • Fewer choices and bigger targets improve completion.
  • Feedback, forgiveness and familiarity beat cleverness.

Information architecture and navigation

Organise content so people can find things without thinking.

  • IA is grouping, labelling and linking.
  • Three to five bottom tabs, search for big catalogues.
  • Test with card sorts and tree tests, not opinions.

Interaction design: flows, states, forms and microcopy

Design the moments between the screens.

  • Design flows and all states, not only the happy path.
  • One primary action per screen; specific microcopy.
  • Reduce form effort with defaults, autofill and the right keyboard.

Visual design and design systems for PMs

Read a screen like a designer and know what a design system buys you.

  • Hierarchy, spacing, contrast and consistency make most of the difference.
  • A design system is tokens, components and patterns with rules.
  • Reuse beats reinvent unless the user value is clear.

Prototyping and usability testing

Test ideas cheaply before engineers build them.

  • Match fidelity to the question.
  • Five users, real tasks, think aloud.
  • Usability tests find why; A/B tests find how much.

Accessibility and inclusive design (with India in mind)

Design for everyone, including low-end phones and many languages.

  • Accessibility benefits all users and the business.
  • Contrast, targets, labels, text scaling are the basics.
  • In India, design for language, low-end devices and low-trust contexts.

E-commerce product design: use cases and how it works

Design discovery, product pages, cart and checkout that convert and build trust.

  • Every stage has a user question to answer.
  • Show total cost and delivery date early; fewer fields at checkout.
  • Measure each stage: search, add-to-cart, checkout, payment success, returns.

Fintech product design: use cases and how it works

Design money moments that feel safe, clear and fast.

  • Money UX is about clarity, trust and honest status.
  • Design the pending and failed states most carefully.
  • Add friction only where risk is high.

Other industries: delivery, mobility, SaaS, health and education

See the same principles applied to different services.

  • The same principles apply across industries.
  • Learn the domain's risks and rules before designing.
  • Reuse proven patterns: search, status, trust, repeat.

Behaviour design, growth and the ethics of persuasion

Use habit and motivation design without manipulating users.

  • Make the right behaviour easy and well-prompted.
  • Habits come from value plus a repeatable loop.
  • Avoid dark patterns; trust is a long-term metric.

Measuring design: metrics, experiments and impact

Prove that design work moves outcomes.

  • Link design changes to outcome metrics and guardrails.
  • Use HEART to cover happiness and task success, not only clicks.
  • Combine experiments with qualitative understanding.
Open the full class →Advanced · Class 15

Scaling design: systems, collaboration and design debt

Run design well across teams and over time.

  • Bring problems and data to design, not solutions.
  • Give feedback as user impact.
  • Manage design debt like tech debt.

AI-era product design

Design AI features that users can trust and control.

  • Set expectations, show sources, keep humans in control.
  • Design for AI mistakes, not only success.
  • Combine chat with structured UI.

Product design interviews: how to answer and what interviewers want

Practise the common formats with a clear structure.

  • Use a clear structure and say it out loud.
  • Choose one user and one problem, justified.
  • Always finish with metrics, risks and next steps.

Final project: a product design case study

Create a portfolio-quality case that shows your thinking.

  • A good case shows problem, evidence, options and impact.
  • Show trade-offs and what you would test.
  • Practise presenting the story in five minutes.

Interview questions

What is product design, and how is it different from UI design?

Product design covers the whole experience: problem, research, structure, flows, interface, content, testing and measurement. UI design is the visual layer inside it.

What is the role of a PM in product design?

Define the problem, users, goals and metrics, set constraints and priorities, enable research and testing, and align design, engineering and business. The PM does not dictate the visuals.

Walk me through your design process.

Discover users and data, define the problem and metric, ideate several options, prototype, test with users, ship, measure and iterate.

How do you decide which problem to solve?

Combine evidence from users and data, size the impact on the business goal, check feasibility and strategic fit, and prioritise by impact versus effort.

Design a savings app for college students.

Clarify scope, pick a user (hostel students), name top pains, prioritise automatic small savings, compare options (round-ups, goals, auto-save), pick one, describe flow and states, and define activation and retention metrics with guardrails.

How would you improve checkout on an e-commerce app?

Look at the funnel, find the biggest drop, show total cost and delivery date earlier, reduce form fields with saved addresses and autofill, offer UPI first, add trust cues, prevent double payment, and measure completion and payment success.

What makes a good fintech UX?

Clarity of money, honest status, security cues, error prevention, plain language, and compliance by design. Handle pending and failed states with care.

A payment failed but money was debited. What should the screen say?

State what happened, that the money is safe, when it returns or will be confirmed, a reference number, and the next action. Keep the order pending until the gateway confirms.
