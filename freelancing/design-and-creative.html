1. Logo Designer

Lesson 1.1

Industry-specific logo systems

  • E-commerce logos. Clean typography, a simple vector icon and high readability at small sizes such as a browser tab, an app icon and a packaging label.
  • Fintech logos. Geometric symmetry, calm trust-building colours (deep blues and greens are common) and abstract marks suggesting growth, safety or flow.
  • System, not one file. Deliver a primary logo, a compact mark, one-colour and reversed versions, clear space and minimum size.
  • Originality. Check that the idea is not a copy of an existing mark; search before you deliver.

Read this lesson with its practice task (PDF) · in the full lesson guide, page 47

Read more (official sites): Brand New ↗

Lesson 1.2

Tooling and workflow

  • Vector tools. Adobe Illustrator and the free Inkscape draw shapes that scale without loss.
  • Font pairing. Pick one display and one text font from Google Fonts or Adobe Fonts; check the licence for commercial use.
  • Animation. After Effects can animate a logo reveal; keep it under five seconds.

Read this lesson with its practice task (PDF) · in the full lesson guide, page 48

Read more (official sites): Inkscape ↗ · Google Fonts ↗ · Adobe Fonts ↗

Lesson 1.3

Asset sourcing and typography

  • Inspiration, not copying. Behance, Dribbble and Brand New show current work and case studies.
  • Fontsource. Open-source fonts packaged for web projects; check each font's licence.
  • Mood boards. Collect references, then write what you will take from them (spacing, colour, mood).

Read this lesson with its practice task (PDF) · in the full lesson guide, page 48

Read more (official sites): Behance ↗ · Dribbble ↗ · Fontsource ↗

2. Graphic Designer

Lesson 2.1

Principles of layout

  • Visual hierarchy. Size, weight, colour and position tell the eye where to look first.
  • Negative space. Empty space gives focus and calm.
  • Colour theory. Use a limited palette with one accent.
  • Contrast. Text must be readable.

Read this lesson with its practice task (PDF) · in the full lesson guide, page 49

Read more (official sites): Adobe colour ↗

Lesson 2.2

Social media, ads and print collateral

  • Canva, Figma and Photoshop. Canva is fast for templates, Figma for shared components, Photoshop for photo work.
  • Sizes. Use each platform's current recommended sizes and safe areas.
  • Print. 300 dpi, bleed and CMYK; ask the printer for specs.

Read this lesson with its practice task (PDF) · in the full lesson guide, page 50

Read more (official sites): Canva ↗ · Figma ↗ · Photoshop ↗

3. Web Designer (UI focus)

Lesson 3.1

Design systems in Figma

  • Components. Reusable elements with variants (primary, secondary, disabled).
  • Auto layout. Frames that resize with their content; check Figma's current documentation for its features.
  • Variables and design tokens. Named values for colour, spacing and type, with modes such as light and dark.
  • Handoff. Name layers clearly and document states.

Read this lesson with its practice task (PDF) · in the full lesson guide, page 51

Read more (official sites): Figma help ↗

4. Video Editor

Lesson 4.1

Timeline editing, J/L cuts, sound design and colour grading

  • Timeline. Cut to the beat of the story; remove every frame that does not help.
  • J and L cuts. The audio from the next shot starts before the picture (J) or the audio of the last shot continues over the next picture (L); they smooth transitions.
  • Sound design. Dialogue first, then effects, then music; clean noise.
  • Colour grading with LUTs. A LUT applies a colour look.

Read this lesson with its practice task (PDF) · in the full lesson guide, page 52

Read more (official sites): DaVinci Resolve ↗ · Premiere Pro ↗

5. Photo Editor and Photoshop Expert

Lesson 5.1

Non-destructive editing, frequency separation and compositing

  • Non-destructive. Use layers, adjustment layers, masks and smart objects so edits can be changed.
  • Frequency separation. Splits colour and tone from fine texture so skin can be smoothed while keeping natural texture.
  • Compositing. Match light direction, colour and perspective.
  • Colour correction. Set a neutral white point first.

Read this lesson with its practice task (PDF) · in the full lesson guide, page 53

Read more (official sites): Photoshop tutorials ↗

6. UX Designer

Lesson 6.1

User research, wireframing, information architecture and usability testing

  • Research. Interview real users to learn goals and problems.
  • Wireframes. Low-fidelity layouts that test structure before visual design.
  • Information architecture. How content is grouped and labelled; test with card sorting.
  • Usability testing. Watch five people try tasks and note where they struggle; Maze and Figma prototypes support remote tests.

Read this lesson with its practice task (PDF) · in the full lesson guide, page 54

Read more (official sites): Maze ↗ · Nielsen Norman Group ↗

7. Presentation Designer

Lesson 7.1

Slide master, data visualisation and pitch decks

  • Slide master. Set fonts, colours and layouts once so every slide matches.
  • Chart clean-up. Remove clutter, label directly, use one highlight colour and a title that states the finding.
  • Pitch deck. Problem, solution, market, product, traction, model, team, ask; one message per slide.

Read this lesson with its practice task (PDF) · in the full lesson guide, page 55

Read more (official sites): PowerPoint help ↗ · Google Slides help ↗

8. Branding Designer

Lesson 8.1

Full brand identity systems

  • Style guide. Logo rules, colours, type, imagery, spacing and examples of right and wrong use.
  • Usage guidelines. Clear space, minimum size and misuse examples.
  • Packaging mockups. Show the identity on real formats; label mockups as concepts.
  • Brand voice. Three traits with do and don't phrases.

Read this lesson with its practice task (PDF) · in the full lesson guide, page 56

Read more (official sites): Brand guidelines examples ↗

9. 3D Modeler

Lesson 9.1

Polygon modelling, PBR texturing, lighting and rendering in Blender

  • Polygon modelling. Build shapes from vertices, edges and faces; keep clean quads.
  • PBR materials. Base colour, roughness, metallic and normal maps describe how a surface reacts to light.
  • Studio lighting. Key, fill and rim lights.
  • Rendering. Choose Cycles for realism or Eevee for speed; denoise and set samples to balance time and quality.
