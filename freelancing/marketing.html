1. Social Media Manager

Lesson 1.1

Daily workflow and operations routine

  • Content calendar. Plan topics, formats, dates and owners; leave room for news.
  • Community engagement. Reply within a set time and escalate complaints.
  • Analytics. Track reach, engagement and clicks in Metricool or Buffer; review weekly and monthly.

Read this lesson with its practice task (PDF) · in the full lesson guide, page 59

Read more (official sites): Metricool ↗ · Buffer ↗

Lesson 1.2

Account onboarding and audit procedures

  • Onboarding. Collect logins safely (use platform roles, not shared passwords), brand assets, goals, audience and approvals.
  • Audit. Review profile, posting frequency, top posts, audience and competitors.
  • Report. Findings, three quick wins and a 30-day plan.

Read this lesson with its practice task (PDF) · in the full lesson guide, page 60

Read more (official sites): Meta Business Help ↗

2. Google Ads Expert

Lesson 2.1

Keyword research, ad groups and match types

  • Intent. Searches show intent: informational, commercial or transactional.
  • Ad groups. One theme per group so ads match searches.
  • Match types. Exact matches the same meaning, phrase matches searches that include the meaning in order, broad matches a wider set of related searches.

Read this lesson with its practice task (PDF) · in the full lesson guide, page 60

Read more (official sites): Google Ads Help ↗

Lesson 2.2

Conversion tracking, Performance Max and bidding

  • Google Tag Manager. Fire a conversion tag when a form is submitted; test with preview mode.
  • Performance Max. An automated campaign type across Google channels; it needs good assets, accurate conversions and patience.
  • Bidding. Target CPA aims for a cost per conversion; Target ROAS aims for return on ad spend.

Read this lesson with its practice task (PDF) · in the full lesson guide, page 61

Read more (official sites): Tag Manager ↗ · Performance Max ↗

3. SEO Expert

Lesson 3.1

On-page optimisation

  • Headers and titles. One clear H1; titles and descriptions that match the content.
  • Schema markup. Structured data (JSON-LD) that describes the page, such as Product or FAQ, so search engines can understand it.
  • Content. Answer the question the searcher asked.

Read this lesson with its practice task (PDF) · in the full lesson guide, page 62

Read more (official sites): Search Central ↗

Lesson 3.2

Technical audits, link building and Search Console

  • Screaming Frog. Crawls a site to find broken links, redirect chains, missing titles and duplicates.
  • Link building. Earn links with useful content and real relationships; do not buy links.
  • Search Console. Shows indexing problems, queries and pages; submit sitemaps.

Read this lesson with its practice task (PDF) · in the full lesson guide, page 63

Read more (official sites): Screaming Frog ↗ · Search Console ↗

4. Facebook and Meta Ads Specialist

Lesson 4.1

Campaign set-up for real estate, tech agencies and e-commerce

  • Objectives. Leads for real estate and agencies; sales for e-commerce.
  • Special categories. Housing, credit, employment and some other categories have special ad rules limiting targeting.
  • Creative and offer. One clear offer per ad and a matching landing page.

Read this lesson with its practice task (PDF) · in the full lesson guide, page 63

Read more (official sites): Meta ads policies ↗

Lesson 4.2

Budget optimisation and ROAS: audiences and the Conversions API

  • Custom audiences. People who already engaged with you, using consented data.
  • Lookalike audiences. New people similar to a source audience.
  • Conversions API (CAPI). Sends events from your server to Meta, which helps measurement when browser tracking is blocked; set it up with consent and avoid duplicate events using event IDs.
  • ROAS. Revenue divided by ad spend.

Read this lesson with its practice task (PDF) · in the full lesson guide, page 64

5. Email Marketer

Lesson 5.1

Deliverability: SPF, DKIM and DMARC

  • SPF. A DNS record that lists the servers allowed to send for your domain.
  • DKIM. A signature added to each email, checked against a public key in DNS.
  • DMARC. A policy that says what to do when SPF or DKIM fails and where to send reports.
  • Honest sending. These records prove who you are; they do not make unwanted email acceptable.

Read this lesson with its practice task (PDF) · in the full lesson guide, page 65

Read more (official sites): Google sender guidelines ↗ · DMARC overview ↗

Lesson 5.2

Outreach infrastructure at scale, safely

  • Tools. Instantly.ai and Smartlead manage multiple mailboxes, warm-up, sequences and reply tracking.
  • Rules before volume. Contact only relevant recipients, follow the anti-spam and privacy laws of your and their country, identify yourself honestly, include an opt-out and honour it at once.
  • Safe practice. Use a separate sending domain, authenticate it, start with small volumes, watch bounces and complaints, and stop if they rise.
  • Job applications. Mass-emailing employers rarely works.
  • Do not. Buy lists, fake sender names or rotate domains to dodge blocks.

Read this lesson with its practice task (PDF) · in the full lesson guide, page 66

Read more (official sites): Instantly ↗ · Smartlead ↗

6. Marketing Automation Consultant

Lesson 6.1

CRM integration, lead scoring and nurture funnels

  • CRM integration. Connect forms, ads and the CRM (HubSpot or GoHighLevel) so each lead has a source.
  • Lead scoring. Add points for fit (role, size) and behaviour (pricing page visits); pass leads to sales at a set score.
  • Nurture. Send helpful emails matched to the stage.

Read this lesson with its practice task (PDF) · in the full lesson guide, page 67

Read more (official sites): HubSpot Academy ↗ · GoHighLevel help ↗

7. Lead Generation Specialist

Lesson 7.1

B2B prospecting, outreach scripts and PPC landing pages

  • Sourcing. Apollo.io and LinkedIn Sales Navigator help find businesses and roles; follow each platform's terms and privacy law, and do not scrape what the terms forbid.
  • Outreach scripts. Short, relevant, with one clear question.
  • PPC landing pages. One offer, a matching headline, proof, a short form and fast loading.
