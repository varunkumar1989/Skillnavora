01What is your favourite product and why?
“Google Maps for commuting. My job is ‘get to work on time with least stress.’ It works because (1) live traffic turns uncertainty into an ETA I trust, (2) it re-routes proactively, (3) it combines modes. A weakness for me: on crowded metro lines it doesn't tell me how full trains are, so I can't choose a less crowded one. I'd test showing crowding levels from anonymised, aggregated data, measuring route-choice changes and satisfaction, with privacy as a guardrail.”

02What makes a product great?
“A great product reliably helps a specific user make progress on an important job, with as little effort and risk as possible, in a way the business can sustain. Signals: users return without being pushed (retention curves flatten), they recommend it, and the economics improve with scale. Example: a payments flow that just works at checkout — invisible reliability is a feature.”

03Critique the onboarding of an app you used recently.
“A budgeting app asked for bank linking, notifications and goals before showing anything useful. Heuristic: visibility and user control — I had no idea what value I'd get. Biggest fix: show a sample dashboard immediately and ask for bank linking in context when I tap ‘see my spending’. Metric: activation (first budget created within 3 days); guardrails: permission grant rate, privacy complaints.”

04Which product do you think is badly designed, and how would you fix it?
“Many parking-payment apps: you must register, add a card and find a zone code while standing in the street. Constraint: municipalities require identification and payment records. Fix: a QR on the meter that opens a lightweight web flow with wallet payment, with optional account creation later. Trade-off: less data capture; mitigate with optional emailed receipts.”

05How would you improve YouTube Shorts?

I'd A/B the topic picker; primary: day-7 return rate of new viewers; guardrails: session-satisfaction surveys and time-spent spikes among younger users.”

06Should Netflix let users stop a title from leaving the catalogue?

“Don't react instantly. Check churn and win/loss by segment, interview switchers, and see whether price or features drive it. Choose based on margin impact and which segment matters strategically.”

35Define activation for a team-collaboration product.

36An A/B test shows +3% conversion with p = 0.04. Ship?
“Check: was this the pre-registered primary metric? Adequate power and full weeks? Any sample ratio mismatch? Guardrails flat? Does the low end of the confidence interval still justify the cost? Is there a novelty risk or a segment that reverses? If all good, ship with a holdout; if not, extend or rerun.”

37Explain p-value and confidence interval to a non-technical stakeholder.
“The p-value tells us how surprising our result would be if the change actually did nothing — small means unlikely to be pure chance. The confidence interval gives the range of effect sizes consistent with our data, like ‘between +1 and +4 points’. It's the range that matters for the business decision.”
Learn it: Analytics →
38How would you calculate retention and what pitfalls exist?
“Group users by signup week; define ‘active’ as a meaningful action; retention in week k = active users in week k ÷ cohort size. Pitfalls: mixed definitions, small cohorts, survivorship bias, seasonality, counting any app open as active.”
Learn it: Analytics →

67What is an API? Explain to a non-technical person.
“An API is like a restaurant menu and waiter: it lists what you can ask for, you place a request in a standard format, and the kitchen sends back the dish — or an error if it's unavailable. Apps use APIs to ask other systems for data or actions, like a weather app requesting today's forecast.”

76When should you NOT use AI for a feature?

77Explain RAG vs fine-tuning.

79Your AI feature hallucinates 3% of the time. Launch or not?
