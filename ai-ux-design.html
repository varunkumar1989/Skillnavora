Designing AI Experiences (AI UX)

Design AI features people trust and use: interaction patterns (copilot, chat, agent, inline), setting expectations, explainability and citations, feedback and control, failure and handoff design, and testing AI UX.

In one line

AI UX is designing products where outputs are probabilistic: helping users understand what AI can do, trust it the right amount, correct it easily, and recover when it's wrong.

Design for uncertainty and the right level of trust.

  • Aim for calibrated trust.
  • Always provide control and recovery.

Interview questions

Key principles of AI UX?

Set expectations, design for uncertainty, transparency with sources, user control and recovery, graceful failure with handoff.

When not to use chat?

When a task is small and in a workflow; an inline button or suggestion is faster and clearer.

How do you build trust?

Citations, plain explanations, labels for AI content, consistent quality, and easy correction.

What feedback signals matter?

Accept/edit/regenerate/abandon rates and edit distance, plus explicit ratings with reasons.

How do you test AI UX?

Wizard-of-Oz, prototypes with real and bad outputs, task success and trust calibration metrics.

Design for an AI agent action?

Preview, confirmation, progress, undo window, and clear logs.

What is different about designing AI interfaces compared with traditional software?

Outputs are probabilistic and open-ended, so design must set expectations, show sources, handle errors, give control and gather feedback, rather than assume the same result every time.

How do you design for hallucinations?

Prevent with grounding and narrow scope, detect with checks and citation requirements, reveal with sources and uncertainty, and recover with easy edits, reporting and human help.
